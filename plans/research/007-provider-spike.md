## Recommendation

For the low-use hackathon clarified in this thread, recommend **Transitous/MOTIS for on-demand routing, with direct MBTA data to qualify predictions and support later watches**, when the real-data work is taken up. Use the existing gazetteer first and Photon’s public demo for occasional additional searches. This is the fastest route to real Boston itineraries without building a graph or obtaining a routing key. It is a **conditional prototype recommendation**, not a claim that the public service is an unrestricted production backend. The current product MVP can use fixtures; plan 007 remains after that milestone and does not block its UI flow.

The durable alternative is **local OpenTripPlanner with MBTA GTFS + GTFS-RT + Bluebikes GBFS**, especially if complete bike/transit combinations or staying private beyond prototyping matter. Local MOTIS is also credible and preserves more of the hosted API shape. Prefer OTP for the fuller parking/rental/bike-on-board path; neither local engine has been benchmarked here. Do not deploy both merely as a precaution.

Important conditions:

- Transitous permits light prototype testing before publishing, but otherwise requires an open-source, noncommercial application. This repository is currently **private**, confirmed through GitHub. Ongoing private use is therefore not cleared. Its policy also asks users to coordinate significant load, many users, and automated requests. Direct MBTA watch checks avoid polling the planner, but automatic missed-trip replanning still needs that policy question resolved or a local engine. No provider was contacted. [Transitous policy](https://transitous.org/api/)
- Google Routes and Google Places are screened out under the standard non-EEA terms because their content cannot be used with a non-Google map. MBTA predictions do not remove that restriction. The settled MapLibre/OpenFreeMap stack need not change. [Routes terms §19](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms), [Places policy](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies)
- No engine makes every prediction truly live. A real sample below demonstrates why `realTime: true` alone is insufficient for MBTA.

## Routing comparison

### Transitous hosted MOTIS: best conditional hackathon starting point

**Confirmed:** the source registry lists MBTA schedules and three realtime sources. The displayed MBTA schedule update was 2026-11-11 02:20 UTC. Credential-free production requests returned Boston transit, street geometry, alerts, scoped stop/trip IDs, scheduled and predicted timestamps. The API returned car and bicycle routes as separate direct options. Five small routing/rental probes were made, without load testing. [MBTA source entry](https://transitous.org/sources/#mbta), [MOTIS API](https://github.com/motis-project/motis/blob/master/openapi.yaml)

MOTIS supports transit plus walking/cycling/rental access and egress, GTFS-RT, and GBFS. Bike carriage can be required. That is engine capability, **not proof that Transitous has Bluebikes configured**. The Boston rental probe returned no station records and an unrelated German provider despite the requested bounds; this probe does not establish Bluebikes presence or absence. Use the directly verified Bluebikes feed for dock pins. Personal-bike parking continuity and legal carriage still need application rules. [MOTIS setup](https://github.com/motis-project/motis/blob/master/docs/setup.md)

**Terms/operations:** no key, published per-request charge, or SLA. Requires identifying User-Agent, visible source link and underlying attribution. Coordinate over 720 plan-type requests/day and automated use; this is a coordination trigger, not a purchased quota. The policy does not give a numeric retention TTL. Retaining attributed itinerary snapshots appears consistent with the open-data service, but this is an inference, not express unlimited retention permission; do not mirror its whole API. Public static data downloads have a separate documented path. [Usage policy](https://transitous.org/api/), [data operations](https://transitous.org/doc/)

**Fit:** strongest no-key prototype candidate; internet/community service dependency and private-project/automation eligibility are the main blockers. No evidence of a Boston latency or availability guarantee. Public routing permits MapLibre use subject to source terms; there is no provider-basemap lock in the reviewed policy.

### Local OpenTripPlanner: strongest complete-mode path

OTP explicitly combines scheduled transit, walking, cycling and rental services. It runs on Windows through a JVM. Use MBTA GTFS for schedules/shapes, GTFS-RT TripUpdates for predictions/cancellations, alerts for disruptions, and GBFS for docks. VehiclePositions are useful for location; they are not a substitute for arrival predictions. No vendor account or routing fee is needed for local open-source routing. OSM and agency data licenses still apply. [OTP overview](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/), [GTFS-RT configuration](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/GTFS-RT-Config/)

**Mode fit:** direct walk/bike/car; transit access/egress; rental bikes; personal bike carried aboard; bicycle parking then transit; car park-and-ride where input parking exists. The current docs use modes such as `BICYCLE_TO_PARK` and `CAR_TO_PARK`; old `BICYCLE_PARK`/`CAR_PARK` examples should not be copied blindly. OSM parking completeness is a constraint, and a known parking location does not imply live space availability. [Routing modes](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/RoutingModes/)

**Integration:** `planConnection` supports departure/arrival search and pagination. Pin an actual release and its schema before coding: latest/dev docs deprecate old leg timing and `realTime` fields in favor of richer time/trip-state objects. Polyline decoding must use that response’s precision. GBFS v1 is supported, relevant to the observed Bluebikes v1.1 feed. [GraphQL query](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/api/dev-2.x/graphql-gtfs/queries/planConnection), [leg schema](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/api/dev-2.x/graphql-gtfs/types/Leg), [GBFS](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/GBFS-Config/)

**Limits:** GTFS bike flags are not the whole agency policy; rental availability is current inventory, not a reservation for a future departure. OTP fares are disabled by default, with GTFS fare configuration and experimental Fares V2 support available. Missing fare data must not become a zero-dollar transit claim. [Fares](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/sandbox/Fares/)

**Fit:** preferred local engine if full mode mix is required now. More setup/maintenance than hosted routing; laptop feasibility remains unmeasured.

### Local MOTIS: real alternative, not just the Transitous brand

Uses local GTFS + OSM, GTFS-RT and GBFS; supports street routing and optional geocoding/tiles. Official instructions include native Windows binaries and PowerShell import/server commands. MIT software license. We need not build tiles or replace OpenFreeMap. Transitous response shapes provide a migration advantage, though API versions and namespace IDs still need pinning. [Repository and Windows instructions](https://github.com/motis-project/motis), [configuration](https://github.com/motis-project/motis/blob/master/docs/setup.md)

**Fit:** credible local alternative if avoiding a JVM or keeping the MOTIS adapter matters. No measured Boston RAM, import time or latency here; do not claim it is lighter than OTP. Full parking continuity, bike rules and Bluebikes routing need validation. Scoped IDs differ between deployments, so cached hosted IDs are not automatically portable.

### Transitland / Interline: credible hosted fallback with an MBTA overlay

Current documentation covers static transit across US operators, with **routing realtime limited to San Francisco Bay Area operators**. Boston therefore needs direct MBTA prediction matching and connection revalidation. It returns walk/transit legs and geometry; separate hosted Valhalla endpoints provide car/bike/foot. Transit routing currently supports walking and transit, not integrated rental/bike-on-board. [Current coverage and modes](https://www.transit.land/documentation/routing-platform/transit-otp/)

API key required. Explorer offers 1,000 routing requests/month for noncommercial testing; a hobbyist/academic application can obtain more, not automatic approval. PAYG includes 10,000 routing units/month, then $2/1,000 units in the first paid band; transit calls use two units, street calls one. [Pricing](https://www.transit.land/plans-pricing), [unit accounting](https://www.transit.land/documentation/plans-and-billing)

Results can be used to create maps/routing outputs, with Transitland and source attribution; reviewed terms do not require a Transitland basemap. Retention duration for saved API itineraries is not explicitly settled in the reviewed material; verify before treating it as unlimited. Terms include restrictions on competitive services and require downstream disclaimers, so suitability for this planner deserves confirmation before choosing it as the long-term backend. [Data terms](https://www.transit.land/terms), [Interline terms](https://www.interline.io/legal/terms/)

**Fit:** a private-project hosted option worth considering if a key is acceptable, but schedule routing plus an overlay can miss a connection that realtime-aware search would have chosen. It is not equivalent to an engine routing directly over realtime.

### Google Routes, with and without MBTA V3

Technical candidate: transit plus walking, and separate drive/walk/bicycle requests with encoded geometry. Transit provides stop names, locations and times, optional total fare, departure/arrival searches and alternatives. It does not provide native bike+transit or park-and-ride composition through a transit request. Transit queries allow up to 100 days forward, subject to schedule changes. [Transit API](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/transit-route)

The response contract does not provide an MBTA GTFS trip ID or a reliable per-leg freshness/uncertainty indicator equivalent to the agency feeds. Google may incorporate agency updates, but **Boston live provenance was not verified**. Matching by stop coordinates/name, route label, direction and time is an inference with ambiguity; stop-name joins alone are unsafe. A V3 overlay can add agency predictions, but cannot repair Google's display license or guarantee that its connection search used those predictions.

**Blocked for this app:** standard Routes terms §19.2 prohibit use with a non-Google map; §19.1 allowing use without a map does not permit MapLibre display. The 30-day coordinate caching exception is not blanket permission to retain full route results in SQLite. Both Google variants therefore trigger plan 007’s terms STOP condition. Billing address/negotiated terms could differ; no such exception is established. [Service terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms)

Billing/API key or OAuth required. Compute Routes default rate limit is 3,000 queries/minute. Current Essentials list price is $5/1,000 after 10,000 free/month; traffic-aware Pro is $10/1,000 after 5,000 free/month. These are per-SKU caps, not the old blanket $200 monthly credit. No authenticated Google probe was made. [Billing](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/routes/usage-and-billing), [prices](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)

## Road routing complements

These can supply street geometry/estimated durations. They do not, by merely replacing a walk leg with a bike leg, establish feasible boarding times, docks, fares or a legal mixed trip.

- **OSRM:** road-only; car/bike/foot profiles require prepared graphs. `profile` in the URL does not dynamically turn a driving graph into a bicycle graph. GeoJSON/polyline output. Strong simple driving complement; separate preparation/update work for each profile. No built-in transit schedules or MBTA realtime. BSD-licensed software, with OSM attribution/data terms for self-hosting. Public demos are not an availability contract. [Official API](https://project-osrm.org/docs/v5.24.0/api/), [repository](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend)
- **Valhalla:** excellent tiled road router with dynamic car/bike/foot costings and polyline6 geometry. It also has transit/multimodal functionality, so calling the engine road-only would be wrong. However, current multimodal docs describe pedestrian+transit and explicitly lack arrive-by support for that costing, a material late-return limitation. A ready Boston realtime transit deployment was not verified. Use it as a road complement here; Windows HTTP-service limitations favor WSL2/container deployment. MIT software. [API](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/route/api-reference/), [Windows compatibility](https://github.com/valhalla/valhalla)
- **GraphHopper:** commercial Directions is a road-routing service; the open-source repository separately has a GTFS transit module and a realtime demonstration. Not accurate to call the whole project road-only. No verified Boston GBFS/full-mode deployment here. Java-based local hosting is an option. Hosted Free is 500 credits/day; Basic currently €69/month with 5,000/day. Map display is allowed; hosted terms permit temporary client caching, not unrestricted server retention or API redistribution. Software is Apache-2.0; local OSM-derived outputs have different terms from hosted results. [Engine](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper), [GTFS module](https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/tree/master/reader-gtfs), [pricing](https://www.graphhopper.com/pricing/), [terms](https://www.graphhopper.com/terms/)
- **openrouteservice / HeiGIT:** road/active-travel directions, not a timetable transit engine. Hosted key required; old API host is deprecated in favor of `api.heigit.org`. Backend FAQ documents a 2,000 directions/day default; the current account-plan page is client-rendered and its exact new-account limits were not independently extracted. Check the actual key’s quota before relying on that number. Retain ORS/HeiGIT/OSM attribution; no provider-basemap requirement established. Exact hosted retention rights should be checked in the account terms. Self-hosting removes hosted request quotas but adds graph maintenance. [Current API](https://api.heigit.org/), [FAQ](https://giscience.github.io/openrouteservice/frequently-asked-questions), [migration announcement](https://ask.openrouteservice.org/t/reducing-the-quota-of-deprecated-api-api-openrouteservice-org/8013)

Recommendation: do not add a second road engine for the first demo. Transitous returned separate car and bike shapes; OTP supports direct street routing. If later road requirements exceed those, compare local Valhalla versus simple OSRM, or a hosted service with explicitly suitable retention terms. None supplies gas price, parking price, live traffic, or bike safety merely because it returns geometry.

Search-as-you-type, proximity bias and typo handling suit the current hook. ## Agency data, identifiers, freshness and costs to travelers

**MBTA:** public GTFS ZIP and GTFS-RT protobuf/JSON endpoints are documented and accessible without a new account. V3 provides stops, trips, schedules, predictions and alerts, not a general door-to-door routing API. Anonymous V3 limit is 20/minute; standard key limit is 1,000/minute, with increases by request. Anonymous limit was observed in response headers; keyed limit is documented, not tested. Conditional GET is supported, and MBTA recommends streaming where appropriate. No per-request price is published in these reviewed developer materials. [Portal](https://api-v3.mbta.com/), [best practices](https://www.mbta.com/developers/v3-api/best-practices), [GTFS](https://github.com/mbta/gtfs-documentation/blob/master/reference/gtfs.md), [GTFS-RT](https://github.com/mbta/gtfs-documentation/blob/master/reference/gtfs-realtime.md)

Preserve a tuple such as **feed namespace/version, GTFS trip ID, service date/start time, route, direction, boarding stop and stop sequence**. Platforms and parent stations differ. Observed Transitous trip `20261114_03:50_us-ma-MBTA_79300386` corresponds to MBTA trip `79300386`, route `171`, stop `64`. That is a verified example, not a universal string-splitting guarantee. Google lacks this direct tuple; Transitland exposes source/feed identifiers that can support a crosswalk. Never infer trip identity solely from the displayed “Red Line.”

Store provider refresh identity durably or in a reversible provider-owned ID scheme before watches ship. An in-memory-only map will lose identity on server restart; current `source` and `Place.code` are insufficient by themselves. This is an integration requirement, not a new user-facing field decision made here.

**Freshness:** distinguish retrieval time, source feed timestamp, trip-update timestamp, and prediction quality. `source.fetchedAt` only describes retrieval. Fetching schedules now does not make them realtime; absence of a prediction does not establish cancellation. Honor explicit canceled/skipped/non-revenue flags and active alerts, and revalidate all connections after updates. Do not just shift every later leg by the first leg’s delay.

**Observed trap:** Transitous returned `realTime: true` for route 171, departing 08:50Z and arriving 09:15Z versus scheduled 09:11Z. Matching MBTA V3 and GTFS-RT both reported departure uncertainty **300** at boarding. MBTA defines bus 300 as no realtime prediction, using a schedule-based adjustment; <300 is valid realtime, 301 is degraded realtime, and >301 is likely invalid. These are agency-specific codes, not probability percentages. Use static timestamps with `scheduled`, or adjusted non-live times with `estimated`; do not set this sample’s boarding provenance to `realtime` merely from the router flag. Endpoint-specific confidence can differ. A single `Leg.timeSource` cannot express every partial-live case. [Official uncertainty semantics](https://github.com/mbta/gtfs-documentation/blob/master/reference/gtfs-realtime.md), [display guidance](https://www.mbta.com/developers/real-time-display-guidelines)

**Bluebikes:** official GBFS discovery resolved to Lyft-hosted v1.1 feeds. Discovery, station status and information reported TTL 60 seconds. `system_id` was `lyft_bos`; station IDs join status/information and are not GTFS stop IDs. Check renting/returning/installed flags, bikes, free docks, last report and feed age. Current dock inventory cannot guarantee inventory hours later. No reservations or onboard capacity guarantees were verified. [Official feed page](https://bluebikes.com/system-data), [discovery](https://gbfs.bluebikes.com/gbfs/gbfs.json)

**Fare/cost:** MBTA currently includes Fares V2 files, explicitly not all products. OTP requires configuration; MOTIS API supports fare output but these transit samples did not supply a usable fare. Treat fare calculations as estimates until products, transfers, media, rider class and service are validated. The official MBTA page navigation displayed subway $2.40/local bus $1.70; the mapped example uses a labeled $2.40 estimate, not a guaranteed ticket quote. Bluebikes pricing feed exposed only an e-bike single ride, $3 unlock + $0.33/minute, taxable: not proof of complete classic/member pricing. Car cost remains distance/MPG × gas price plus separately estimated parking/tolls; no router turns those into live prices. [MBTA fares](https://www.mbta.com/fares), [recorded rental pricing](samples/007-system-pricing.json)

**Data rights:** MassDOT permits data combination subject to its developer license, requires acknowledgement, and prohibits misrepresentation/unauthorized marks; it offers no accuracy/availability guarantee. The official PDF was downloaded; its indexed official text was reviewed, not a full local PDF extraction. Bluebikes licenses lawful in-product use royalty-free but restricts standalone dataset redistribution, unauthorized access, rider reidentification and trademark use. ## Mode mix, rail and late-night behavior

- **Walk/transit:** all serious transit candidates cover access/transfers/egress conceptually. Boston route geometry is actually verified only for Transitous in this spike. Walk distances, stair/elevator routing and wheelchair limits still depend on input completeness and current alerts.
- **Bike + transit:** distinguish a carried personal bike, a personal bike left at a station, and a rental returned before boarding. A parked bike/car must be retrieved on return. Replacing a walk leg with a quicker bike estimate without rerouting can produce an impossible or illegal itinerary. OTP has the strongest documented complete composition here; MOTIS is a credible mixed-mode alternative, with Boston Bluebikes integration unverified.
- **MBTA rules, fetched directly:** folded devices allowed across services when fully folded. Non-folding bikes prohibited on Green/Mattapan; Red/Orange exclude weekdays 07:00–10:00 and 16:00–19:00. Blue differs: inbound 07:00–09:00, outbound 16:00–18:00. Bus/Silver Line use racks with capacity limits. Some commuter lines allow bikes on all trains; others depend on the timetable’s bike symbol. Ferry captain may limit numbers. Station/platform prohibitions and disruption exceptions also apply. Do not apply one generic rush-hour rule to all rail. [Current MBTA rules](https://www.mbta.com/bikes)
- **Rail classification:** retain GTFS route type and agency internally even though UI mode is `rail`. Tram/light rail, subway, commuter/regional and intercity services have different operating patterns and prediction availability. - **Late return:** a Transitous arrive-by query for South Station → Kendall/MIT at 03:00 local returned overnight scheduled candidates, including a 01:55 → 02:33 option using subway and bus. ## Field mapping into the current contract

Real provider snapshots are under `plans/research/samples/`, deliberately **not** `apps/server/src/providers/__fixtures__/`: the explicit research-only instruction supersedes the old plan’s application-fixture location. [Derived complete itinerary example](samples/007-mapped-itinerary-example.json) uses the first scheduled Red Line result. It is illustrative mapping, not a new application mapper or schema test.

Mapping rules:

1. `itineraries[]` becomes candidates; MOTIS `direct[]` supplies independent street-only candidates, not necessarily mixed transit legs. Do not overlook either array.
2. `startTime`/`endTime` → itinerary/leg `departAt`/`arriveAt`; duration seconds ÷ 60 → minutes. Preserve seconds/fractional minutes internally. Itinerary elapsed time includes waits; it is not always the sum of moving-leg durations.
3. `from/to.lon,lat` → `Place.coordinates: [lon,lat]`; name → name. Validated namespace crosswalk → raw agency stop ID in `Place.code`, with kind `stop`/`station`; retain parent separately when needed.
4. `WALK` → `walk`; `BIKE` → `bike`; rental flags/modes → `bikeshare`; bus/coach → `bus`; subway/tram/regional rail → `rail`; ferry → `ferry`; `CAR` → `car`. Retain underlying subtype for rules. Do not infer rental from the presence of a bike mode alone.
5. `legGeometry.points` decoded at its supplied `precision` → GeoJSON LineString, swapping decoded lat/lon to [lon,lat]. Here precision was **6**, not the commonly assumed 5. First transit point was `[-71.0856,42.362354]`; legs decoded to 3, 46 and 2 points. Missing/degenerate geometry must be surfaced, not replaced with a fabricated road path.
6. Transit schedules → `scheduled`; genuine fresh predictions → `realtime`; street estimates and schedule-adjusted non-live predictions → `estimated`. Omit `delayMinutes` unless realtime; use boarding departure minus scheduled boarding departure in minutes. Different arrival delay must not silently replace that departure delay. Stale source status needs an explicit warning because the current schema has no staleness field.
7. `displayName`/route names → `routeName`; `agencyName` → `agency`; optional distance only when provided. Keep exact provider identity separately for refresh. Research IDs in the example deliberately do not solve watch identity.
8. `transfers` → transfers; sum walk-leg minutes → `walkMinutes`. Transfer buffer on the next boarding leg should represent available connection slack **after transfer movement**. The plan’s “next departure minus previous transit arrival” includes the transfer walk and can overstate safety. Existing risk logic examines the immediately previous leg, so a transfer-walk-separated bus also needs reconciliation during integration. No risk code changed here.
9. Provider fare products → USD cost lines only when semantics are understood; de-duplicate tickets covering several legs. Missing fare → documented estimated line(s), never an implicit free fare. Existing `planTrip` computes risk/violations/ranking; source is provider plus retrieval time.

The Red Line example departs 14:03Z, walks one minute, rides 14:04–14:10Z, and walks to 14:11Z: eight minutes total, six on rail, two walking, zero transfers, scheduled rail. Raw response and derived output remain clearly distinguished.

## Request volume and monthly costs

All dollar amounts below are calculations from verified list prices, not bills or measurements. Assume 30 days, USD except stated euros; exclude tax, Gemini, internet, existing hardware and developer time. The user clarified actual hackathon traffic will be low: **1,000 plans/day is a future sizing scenario, not forecast demand**.

### Per-plan assumptions

- Basic: one outbound transit search and two geocoding calls, one per selected endpoint. This is 30,000 routing + 60,000 geocoding requests/month with no cache hits assumed.
- Expanded comparison: 10% request one return search; 20% request a separate drive search. Then 33,000 transit + 6,000 street = **39,000 routing calls/month**, averaging 1.3/plan. A provider returning direct alternatives in the same call may reduce this; extra pages, mode combinations, agent revisions, retries and bike-savings comparisons increase it.
- Search-as-you-type sensitivity: four network queries per endpoint instead of one means eight/plan, or **240,000/month**, before cache savings. Debouncing alone does not guarantee one call. Do not geocode watches repeatedly.
- Watches: illustrative 10% of daily plans become watched, each active for a two-hour window. This is 100 new watches/day, not 100 permanently active users. One batched prediction request per watched trip per check is assumed; two separately fetched transit legs double it. Client GET polling of our saved-trip API need not trigger upstream requests.

### Watch sensitivity: examples, not selected intervals

For each two-hour watched trip: 15-minute checks = 8 calls; 5-minute = 24; 2-minute = 60; 1-minute = 120. At 100 watched trips/day, upstream totals are respectively **24,000 / 72,000 / 180,000 / 360,000 per month**. Ramping 90 minutes at 5-minute spacing plus 30 minutes at 1-minute spacing gives 48 checks/trip, or **144,000/month**. Boundary checks, retries, replans and multiple legs can add requests.

Formula: monthly checks = `30 × watchesStartedPerDay × sum(stageMinutes / intervalMinutes) × requestsPerCheck`, rounded for stage boundaries. This illustrates sensitivity only. Flight stages are not applied to bus/rail. Their exact policies remain open.

At peak, 100 simultaneously due watches at a one-minute interval require 100 requests/minute if each uses one request: above anonymous MBTA’s 20/minute, below the documented keyed 1,000/minute. A demo with four such watches is very different. Batch identical stop/route requests, reuse fresh data, honor 429/backoff and conditional GET, and consider server-side feed ingestion/streaming rather than one full replan per watch.

For feed ingestion, an illustrative one-minute poll of three shared MBTA feeds is 4,320 requests/day (129,600/month), independent of watch count; two GBFS feeds add 2,880/day (86,400/month). These are fixed download/parse/network workloads, not per-plan charges. Bluebikes reported TTL 60 seconds; TTL is not a decision on watch polling. Running feeds only during occasional tests reduces this sharply.
